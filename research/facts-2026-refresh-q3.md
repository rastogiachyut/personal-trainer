## 1. Drop-Set Training — Time-Efficient & Equally Effective 🟢

**Havers, Micke, Geisler & Held (2026), Sports Med Open — meta-analysis, 12 studies, n=274**
- Drop-sets produce hypertrophy (SMD 0.04) and strength (SMD −0.04) gains **statistically identical** to traditional sets
- Drop-sets acutely raise RPE (SMD 1.62) and lactate (SMD 0.67) — they *feel* much harder, and recovery cost may be higher
- Practical: use drop-sets to cut session time without losing gains; don't stack them on every set — they add perceptual/fatigue load
- **Relevance: HIGH** — Achyut's gym sessions are ~45 min; drop-sets on last set of isolation work = same gains, less time

## 2. Training to Failure Is NOT Necessary — Non-Failure Slightly Better for Strength 🟢

**Wu et al. (2026), BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil — meta-analysis, 20 studies, n=556**
- Non-failure training **beat** failure training for dynamic strength (SMD 0.24, p=0.01)
- No difference in hypertrophy, isometric strength, endurance, or power
- Practical: stop 1-3 reps in reserve on compounds; failure only on isolation if at all
- **Relevance: HIGH** — validates the existing RIR-based approach; failure training adds fatigue without extra growth

## 3. Advanced Training Methods ≈ Traditional Training (Big Network Meta-Analysis) 🟢

**Cowley et al. (2026), Sports Med — Bayesian network meta-analysis, 8 methods compared**
- No advanced method (supersets, clusters, drop-sets, flywheel, rest-redistribution, etc.) consistently beat traditional RT for strength, power, hypertrophy, or sprint in untrained-to-moderately-trained people
- One nuance: **rest redistribution schemes** may benefit strength more in **females**; flywheel best for jump adaptation
- Practical: pick methods for time-efficiency/adherence, not because they're "superior" — consistency and effort win
- **Relevance: MODERATE** — reassuring: the simple Day A/B/C plan is not leaving gains on the table

## 4. Intermittent Fasting + Resistance Training Does Not Hurt Gains 🟡

**Zhang et al. (2026), Front Nutr — multilevel meta-analysis, 11 studies, n=303**
- IF + RT produced adaptations broadly similar to RT alone (g = 0.09, CI −0.23 to 0.40) when RT and nutrition are maintained
- No clear differences in max strength, endurance, hypertrophy, or explosive performance
- Caveat: most studies in young adults; evidence quality limited
- **Relevance: MODERATE** — if Achyut prefers a feeding window for calorie control, it won't sabotage muscle

## 5. BFR Is for LOW Loads Only — Adding BFR to Heavy Training Does Nothing 🟢

**Mckee et al. (2026), J Strength Cond Res 40(7) — meta-analysis, 8 chronic + 34 acute studies**
- High-load (≥60% 1RM) training + BFR does **not** improve strength (g=0.11) or size (g=0.08) vs high-load alone — very-low certainty but consistent
- Interesting acute finding: wide cuffs at high pressure may **increase concentric bar velocity** — potential power-training tool
- Practical: keep BFR for light-load/joint-friendly work (as already in the KB); don't add cuffs to heavy sets
- **Relevance: MODERATE** — refines the existing BFR facts: BFR's value is specifically at 15-30% 1RM

## 6. High-Load BFR for Knee Osteoarthritis (Rehab Context) 🟡

**Machado et al. (2026) — overview of systematic reviews**
- BFR therapy improves quadriceps strength/function in knee OA where high-intensity RT is poorly tolerated
- **Relevance: LOW-MODERATE** — relevant if knee issues return at high body weight

## 7. Morning Protein Alone Doesn't Build Muscle (Older Adults, No Exercise) 🟡

**Ikeda et al. (2026), Food Sci Nutr — meta-analysis**
- Protein at breakfast without exercise does not improve muscle mass/strength in older adults
- Reinforces: protein timing is secondary; protein + training is the unit that matters
- **Relevance: LOW** (different population) but myth-busts "anabolic breakfast" marketing

## 8. Age-Related Anabolic Resistance Is Real but Modest 🟡

**Kristiansen et al. (2026), Front Physiol — systematic review + meta-analysis**
- Anabolic resistance (blunted MPS response to protein/exercise) contributes to age-related muscle loss, but magnitude is smaller than often claimed
- **Relevance: LOW** now, useful context for long-term training

3. **Drop-sets are the time-crunch tool** — same gains, half the time, but budget extra recovery
4. **BFR ≠ always beneficial** — its niche is low loads only
