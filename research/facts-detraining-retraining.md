## Timeline of Losses (Younger Adults, <65)

### DT-001 — Strength is mostly unaffected for ~4 weeks of no training
Maximal strength shows trivial losses (g < 0.2) for up to 28 days of training cessation. Losses accelerate after ~1 month.
- **Evidence:** 🟢 STRONG — Bosquet et al. 2013 meta-analysis, 103 studies
- **Practical:** A 2-4 week break costs almost nothing in strength. Don't panic-restart.

### DT-002 — Strength endurance is lost faster than maximal strength
High-rep performance (≥6RM, muscular endurance) declines sooner and harder than 1RM strength, because aerobic/anaerobic conditioning (mitochondria, capillary density, enzymes, blood volume) de-adapts faster than contractile tissue.
- **Evidence:** 🟢 STRONG — Bosquet et al. 2013
- **Practical:** Expect higher-rep sets to feel disproportionately hard when returning.

### DT-003 — Muscle "flatness" in week 1 is glycogen/water, not tissue loss
Early perceived shrinkage = loss of muscle edema, glycogen, and blood flow — not actual contractile protein loss. True tissue loss is much slower than people fear.
- **Evidence:** 🟡 MODERATE — mechanistic consensus from whole-muscle vs fiber CSA studies
- **Practical:** Don't judge muscle loss by the mirror in the first weeks.

### DT-004 — Whole-muscle size declines near baseline after 7-20 weeks off, but fiber CSA is preserved well
Measures of whole-muscle mass/thickness drop non-significantly within 20 days, then trend toward baseline by 7-20 weeks. Muscle fiber cross-sectional area holds up much better — suggesting real tissue loss is slower than scans imply.
- **Evidence:** 🟡 MODERATE — multiple primary studies (Staron; Psilander 2019; Ogasawara; Taaffe & Marcus)

### DT-005 — Older adults (≥65) lose ~2x faster; younger adults get a ~1-month grace period
Pooled effect sizes: older g=0.76 vs younger g=0.31 for strength; losses may start accelerating at ~2 weeks in older adults. Achyut (20s) falls in the protected group.
- **Evidence:** 🟢 STRONG — Bosquet et al. 2013 sub-analyses

## Muscle Memory

### DT-006 — Muscle memory is real and cellular, not just "motor pattern relearning"
Three mechanisms: (1) myonuclear permanence — nuclei added during prior training survive detraining, so regrowth is fast because each nucleus oversees a smaller-than-max domain; (2) epigenetic memory (Seaborne 2018 — methylation changes from prior training persist); (3) resensitization of anabolic signaling pathways after a break.
- **Evidence:** 🟢 STRONG — Egner 2013 (paradigm-shift testosterone/myonuclei study); Gundersen myonuclei line; Seaborne 2018; Snijders/Hornberger reviews

### DT-007 — Regaining takes roughly HALF the layoff duration (range 1/3 to 2/3)
Best estimate from retraining studies: recovery of lost strength/muscle ≈ ½ the cessation length. E.g., 12 weeks off → ~6 weeks back (range 4-8). Seaborne: 7wk train/7wk off/7wk retrain → subjects ended retraining stronger than before detraining.
- **Evidence:** 🟢 STRONG — Henwood & Taffe (24wk off, 12wk retrain regained all); Psilander (20wk off, 5wk retrain); Taaffe & Marcus (12wk off, 6wk to regain); Ogasawara (cyclical 6wk on/3wk off = same 6-month gains as continuous)
- **Practical for Achyut: ~7 weeks off (Jul 9 → Aug 24) → expect to be back at July strength in ~3-5 weeks of consistent training.**

### DT-008 — Periodic 3-week breaks don't reduce long-term gains
Ogasawara: 6 weeks on / 3 weeks off cycling for 6 months produced identical bench 1RM and pec/triceps CSA gains vs training 6 months straight.
- **Evidence:** 🟢 STRONG — RCT (Ogasawara et al. 2013)

## Mitigation (If You Can't Train)

### DT-009 — 1/9th of training volume maintains muscle & strength in young adults
Bickel 2011: after 16 weeks of training, young lifters maintained strength and muscle for 32 weeks on just 1/9th of original volume (older adults needed 1/3rd). One single set per exercise retains gains.
- **Evidence:** 🟢 STRONG — RCT (Bickel et al. 2011); confirmed by Antunes 2021 (1 set/exercise maintained strength + lean mass in older women)
- **Practical:** Even ONE light session/week of push-ups, pull-ups, split squats, hip thrusts (30-45 min) would have largely prevented losses.

### DT-010 — Maintenance floor: protein 1.3-1.4 g/kg LEAN mass + no big deficit/surplus
During no-training periods, keep protein ~1.3-1.4 g/kg lean mass and calories near maintenance. Extreme protein intakes and supplements have NO proven muscle-retention effect without a training stimulus.
- **Evidence:** 🟡 MODERATE — nutrition consensus, cited reviews

### DT-011 — Bed rest/immobilization is a different beast: ~1% strength loss/day
Complete immobilization (injury, illness) loses strength >1%/day and muscle ~0.5%/day — vastly worse than normal detraining. Not applicable here (Achyut is ambulatory), but important distinction.
- **Evidence:** 🟢 STRONG — immobilization literature (e.g., PMC6548068)

## Return-to-Training Protocol (Evidence-Based)

### DT-012 — Week 1 back: full exercise selection & set/rep structure at ~1/3 of previous loads
Run your normal program (same exercises, sets, reps) but at ~33% of pre-layoff weights. A single light session (even 10% max force) dramatically attenuates DOMS, strength loss, and damage markers on subsequent ramp-up. Purpose: prevent crippling soreness from derailing habit re-establishment.
- **Evidence:** 🟢 STRONG — repeated-effect data (nociceptive attenuating exercise), Nuckols protocol

### DT-013 — Week 2 back: autoregulate at 3-5 reps in reserve (RIR)
Week 2: choose weights challenging but not hard — ≥5 RIR first set, ≥3-4 RIR last set. Expect unpredictable set-to-set performance as motor patterns de-rust. Don't test limits in week 2; injury risk is elevated when re-introducing intense training after time off.
- **Evidence:** 🟡 MODERATE — autoregulation literature + injury-risk studies (23160274, 17710181)

### DT-014 — Week 3+: linear weekly load jumps = deficit ÷ (recovery weeks − 1)
Formula: (pre-layoff working weight − current working weight) ÷ (weeks-to-recover − 1) = weekly load increase. Example: 405→255 lb deficit, 6-week recovery → +30 lb/week. When weekly progression stalls, retraining phase is over → resume normal programming.
- **Evidence:** 🟡 MODERATE — derived from retraining time-course studies

### DT-015 — Layoffs <1 month = just a 20% deload; layoffs >1 year = treat as beginner
Under 4 weeks off: reduce loads ~20% for one week, then resume normal training. Over 1 year: run standard linear progression (add 5-10 lb/wk lower body, 2.5-5 lb/wk upper body). Avoid regaining strength fast on low volume while tendons stay deconditioned — recondition tissue with loads, not after them.
- **Evidence:** 🟡 MODERATE — expert consensus (Nuckols/Zourdos/Eure)
